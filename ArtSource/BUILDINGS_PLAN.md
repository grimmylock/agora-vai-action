# Buildings that feel lived in: plan (2026-11-24, Yan: "all of them at least furnished accordingly"; nothing built yet)

**Today** (`BUILDINGCENSUS=1 bash runtest.sh ...`): 149 enterable buildings and 41 solid blocks without a door on the six tier maps (many of the 41 are map-edge walls and the big perimeter masses; the small 5x4 - 7x6 ones are sheds and ruins you cannot enter). The floor under the middle of the enterable buildings: Floor 78, Concrete 60, Dirt 6, LabTile 2, Sand 1, Gravel 1, Asphalt 1 (92 % two kinds). Interiors come from `BuildingCarver` (generic rooms, furniture from `lockers, desk, armchair, crate, crate`) then `Furnish` (wall rules). 14 authored `Stamp` plans exist (`ArtSource/plans`).

**Yan's decisions:** match the game's current art style (props like the atlas desk/armchair/lockers; the six Pixel Lab props of 9ei set the style anchor); every building furnished for what it is (a house has a kitchen and a bathroom, a warehouse has boxes); loot must make sense (no weapons or armor in civilian homes); search for references; do not change maps yet, he reviews every map by hand first; maps keep their size and coordinates.

**Plan**
1. Archetypes: house, shop, bar, clinic, barracks, garage/workshop, warehouse, office, school, church, plant/factory floor, hut/shed. Chosen from the landmark's name, the region and the building's size. Each sets wall look, floor, room list and furniture sets.
2. Room roles on a floor plan: graph of rooms (entrance/public first, then private ones attached; kitchen next to living room, bathroom off a corridor), after the grammar-style approaches in the references below.
3. Furniture sets as vignettes with relation rules (bed + bedside table + wardrobe; counter run + stove + sink; table + chairs; shelves along a wall; workbench + tool rack; stacked crates in aisles). Wall rules and clearance as `Furnish` has them, plus door/path clearance checks.
4. Floors per archetype and room (planks, tile, linoleum, concrete, dirt, worn carpet) + decals (stains, rubble, leaves).
5. Hand-authored plans for the landmark buildings (story places, hub, barracks, village), generator for the rest.
6. Story props: overturned chairs, ransacked drawers, notes, faction signs that fit who lived there.
7. Exteriors: porches, L-shapes, roof variety, signs, fences; and decide for the 41 solid blocks: open the real buildings (sheds/ruins with a ruined interior) or keep a few as intentional ruins.
8. New props (Pixel Lab, same anchor and size band as the atlas): bed, wardrobe, kitchen counter, stove, sink, toilet, bathtub, fridge, table + chairs, shelves, sofa, workbench, tool rack, cash register, school desks, pews, hospital bed, stacked crates/pallets.
9. Loot by building type (Yan: "has to make sense"). Today stashes are fixed at about 8 per map (`Stashes.cs`, Batch 126: "ordinary loot comes from bodies and quests") and the interactive objects are only a footlocker, a PDA, a lock and a blast door. So furniture is decoration by default; some pieces per building become small searchable containers whose pool is set by archetype and furniture kind: house = food, drink, bandages, clothes, tools, small cash, notes, keys; shop = its goods; clinic = medicine; garage = tools, parts, fuel; barracks/armory = ammo, weapons, armor (the only places for them). A harness check fails any weapon/armor entry in a civilian pool.
10. Process: a building census as the metric (this document's numbers), three test buildings (house, shop, barracks) rendered and approved by Yan before any rollout, then one map at a time so his manual map review fits, harness checks (every room reachable, doors clear, deterministic, loot pools legal), WORK_LOG per map.

**Decided (Yan, 2026-11-24):** the small solid blocks (sheds and ruins, about 5x4 to 7x6) become real enterable buildings with a ruined interior; a few stay solid on purpose as ruins (chosen per map during his manual map review).

**Cautions:** furniture is cover (set cover heights on purpose); more props = more sprites per frame (watch `[PERF]`); new landmark names need pt-BR lines.

**References** (design, not code to copy)
- Environmental storytelling as set dressing and purposeful clutter: Game Maker's Toolkit "How Level Design Can Tell a Story" (transcript: https://amara.org/subtitles/Lv64YfjMFhOC/en/1/download/How%20Level%20Design%20Can%20Tell%20a%20Story%20%20Game%20Makers%20Toolkit.en.txt), Game Design Skills on environmental storytelling (https://gamedesignskills.com/game-design/environmental-storytelling/), Cubebrush "Storytelling in Environment Art & Level Design" (https://www.cubebrush.co/blog/storytelling-in-environment-art-level-design).
- Believability = functional logic, dozens of small lived-in items, modular kits (Coohom: https://www.coohom.com/article/how-professional-game-studios-design-3d-interior-environments).
- Function-first floor plans (public rooms first, each gets a function, private rooms attach): Martin 2006 graph grammar as summarised in TU Wien's Spitaler thesis (https://www.cg.tuwien.ac.at/research/publications/2015/spitaler-2015-pbi/spitaler-2015-pbi-thesis.pdf) and Chalmers "Procedural Generation of Indoor Environments" (https://www.cse.chalmers.se/~uffe/xjobb/Lars Rinde o Alexander Dahl-Procedural Generation of Indoor Environments.pdf).
- Furniture as shape-grammar patterns per room type (a dorm always has beds, desks, chairs, closets, then random extras): https://cgl.uwaterloo.ca/CS888/F2008/Projects/c23zhang.
- Project Zomboid: buildings as rooms with a type and loot tables per room type (RoomDef/BuildingDef docs https://projectzomboid.com/modding/zombie/iso/RoomDef.html, room defines and item spawns https://pzfans.com/project-zomboid-room-defines-and-item-spawns/); its players' main complaint about hand-placed interiors is cramped, over-collidable furniture.
